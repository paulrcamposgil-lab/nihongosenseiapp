Grammar

The grammar points of lesson 2 and their examples, as the app explains them.

これ / それ / あれ / どれ — This / that / that over there / which one

Imagine two circles on the floor: one around you, one around the person you're talking to. Something inside your circle is これ. Inside theirs, それ. Outside both, あれ. And どれ is the finger hovering over a row of things: "which one?". These four are complete nouns, so they take は straight away.

  • これは ハチの かさです。Kore wa Hachi no kasa desu.This is the Hachi umbrella.
  • ケンタさん、それは なんですか。Kenta-san, sore wa nan desu ka.Kenta, what's that (in your hand)?
  • あれは ゆうびんきょくです。Are wa yūbinkyoku desu.That over there is the post office.

これ / それ / あれ are for things. Used for a person who can hear you, they sound like «this thing» and come across as rude: use この / その / あの + noun (point 2). And careful with どれ: it takes が, not は (どれが…); lesson 8 explains why.

この / その / あの / どの + Noun — this … / that … / that … over there / which …

Same circles as in point 1, but now the word is glued to a noun, like a prefix: この かさ, その かばん, あの じてんしゃ. They can't stand alone; a noun always follows. They're also the respectful way to point at people: あの ひと would be rude with あれ.

  • この かさは ごひゃくえんです。Kono kasa wa gohyaku en desu.This umbrella is 500 yen.
  • その さいふは ハナさんの さいふですか。Sono saifu wa Hana-san no saifu desu ka.Is that wallet (by you) Hana's?
  • あの じてんしゃは いくらですか。Ano jitensha wa ikura desu ka.How much is that bike over there?

✗ この は… / ✗ これ かさ… Tip: if a noun follows, it ends in の; if not, it ends in れ. With どの + noun, just like with どれ, the particle is が: どの じてんしゃが ケンタさんの じてんしゃですか。

ここ / そこ / あそこ / どこ — here / there / over there / where

The family's third branch is about places. Keep the same two circles: ここ (my spot), そこ (your spot), あそこ (out there), どこ (where?). For now you answer "where is X?" by pointing; street directions arrive in Lesson 4.

  • ノアさん、コンビニは どこですか。Noa-san, konbini wa doko desu ka.Noa, where's the convenience store?
  • メニューは そこです。Menyū wa soko desu.The menu is right there (by you).
  • ハナさんの かばんは ここです。Hana-san no kaban wa koko desu.Hana's bag is here.

To say where something is, Japanese needs nothing new: place + です and you're done.

だれの Noun — whose …

You already know N の N. For "whose?", drop the question word だれ ("who") into the first slot: だれの + noun. To answer, swap だれ for the owner's name. That's all.

  • これは だれの じしょですか。Kore wa dare no jisho desu ka.Whose dictionary is this?
  • ケンタさんの じしょです。Kenta-san no jisho desu.It's Kenta's dictionary.
  • あの ぼうしは だれの ぼうしですか。Ano bōshi wa dare no bōshi desu ka.Whose hat is that over there?

Without の it doesn't work: ✗ だれ ペンですか → ○ だれの ペンですか.

Noun も — … too / also

も is は's twin for "me too" moments: when a second person or thing shares what was just said, it takes も instead of は. It sticks to the thing that shares, not to the end of the sentence.

  • ノアさんは にじゅういっさいです。ケンタさんも にじゅういっさいです。Noa-san wa nijūissai desu. Kenta-san mo nijūissai desu.Noa is 21. Kenta is 21 too.
  • あの かさも ごひゃくえんです。Ano kasa mo gohyaku en desu.That umbrella over there is 500 yen too.
  • ハナさんは かいしゃいんです。ニコさんも かいしゃいんです。Hana-san wa kaishain desu. Niko-san mo kaishain desu.Hana works for a company. Nico does too.

も takes the place of は; the two never go together (✗ はも). And it needs two different subjects: to add another fact about the same person, も doesn't work.

Noun じゃないです — is not …

To say "X is not Y", replace です with じゃないです. That's what you hear in everyday conversation. In a formal meeting you'll hear じゃありません, and in writing ではありません. Like です, it needs a noun right in front.

  • ハナさんは だいがくいんせいじゃないです。Hana-san wa daigakuinsei ja nai desu.Hana is not a grad student.
  • いいえ、けいざいじゃないです。アジアけんきゅうです。Iie, keizai ja nai desu. Ajia kenkyū desu.No, it’s not economics. It’s Asian studies.
  • これは ニコの かさじゃないです。ノアのです。Kore wa Niko no kasa ja nai desu. Noa no desu.This isn't Nico's umbrella. It's Noa's.

When you answer "no", repeat the noun: いいえ、がくせいじゃないです (いいえ + じゃないです alone isn't enough). And careful: たかい and おいしい aren't nouns, so they aren't negated with じゃないです; their negative comes in Lesson 5.

〜ね / 〜よ — sentence endings ne / yo

Think of ね as handing the sentence to the listener for a nod: "…, right?". Think of よ as handing them a piece of news: "…, you know / trust me". Both ride on the very end, after です.

  • にせんえん！ たかいですね。Nisen en! Takai desu ne.2,000 yen! Expensive, isn't it?
  • ここの とんかつは おいしいですよ。Koko no tonkatsu wa oishii desu yo.The tonkatsu here is great, trust me.
  • あれは ぎんこうじゃないですよ。ゆうびんきょくです。Are wa ginkō ja nai desu yo. Yūbinkyoku desu.That's not a bank, you know. It's the post office.

ね asks for agreement; よ gives information. If you use よ about something the other person knows better than you, it sounds like an unrequested lecture.
