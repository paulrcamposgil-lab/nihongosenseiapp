Grammar

The grammar points of lesson 3 and their examples, as the app explains them.

Verb conjugation

Every verb lives in one of three families: ru-verbs, u-verbs and the two irregulars. The dictionary lists the plain form (いく); polite speech uses the ます form (いきます) and its negative ません (いきません). Ru-verbs are the easy family: cut the final る, add ます/ません. U-verbs shift their last kana one row up the vowel chart, from the "u" row to the "i" row (む→み, す→し, く→き, る→り), then add ます/ません. The irregulars are just two, so memorise them: する→します, くる→きます.

  • ハチは よく ねます。Hachi sleeps a lot.
  • ケンタさんは ほっかいどうに かえります。Kenta is going back to Hokkaido.
  • ゴマは ぜんぜん ねません。Goma never sleeps.

The trap is かえる: it ends in -eru but it's a u-verb (かえります). Make a short "black list" of these false friends as you meet them, and check every new -iru/-eru verb against it.

Verb types and the "present tense"

The ます form isn't really "present"; think of it as "not past". So the same sentence can describe a routine (Hana drinks coffee every day) or a plan (Nico goes to the bank tomorrow). Time words in the sentence tell you which.

  • ハナさんは まいにち コーヒーを のみます。Hana drinks coffee every day. (habit)
  • ニコは あした ぎんこうに いきます。Nico will go to the bank tomorrow. (plan)
  • ゴマは やさいを たべません。Goma doesn't eat vegetables.

«I'm reading right now» is NOT this form; that comes in Lesson 7.

Particles を, で, に, へ

Particles are luggage tags: each noun wears one so you know its job. を (read «o») tags the thing the action hits: what you eat, read, watch. で tags the stage where the action takes place. に tags the arrival point of いく, くる, かえる, and also a point on the clock or calendar (see 4). へ (read «e») can stand in for に only as the arrival point, never for times.

  • ケンタさんは がっこうで しんぶんを よみます。Kenta reads the newspaper at school.
  • ケンタさんは どようびに ほっかいどうへ かえります。Kenta goes back to Hokkaido on Saturday.
  • きむらさんは ニコの うちに きます。Mr. Kimura comes to Nico's place.

Ask the verb: "do something there" or "get there"? Doing → で; getting there → に/へ. ✗としょかんに べんきょうします → としょかんで べんきょうします.

Time references

Ask yourself: is this time a fixed point you could circle on a calendar or a clock? Then add に: どようびに, 七時(しちじ)に. Is it a "moving" word that depends on today (きょう, あした, こんばん), a repeat (まいにち, まいばん) or the question いつ? Then no に. あさ and しゅうまつ sit in between: both are fine. For "around", swap に for ごろ (or ごろに).

  • ゴマは まいにち 六時(ろくじ)に おきます。Goma gets up at six every day.
  • ハナさんは 一時(いちじ)ごろ ねます。Hana goes to bed around one.
  • ケンタさんは こんばん うちで えいがを みます。Kenta is watching a movie at home tonight.

✗あしたに / ✗まいにちに / ✗いつに. If there's no number and no day name, it probably doesn't take に.

〜ませんか (invitations)

Japanese invites by asking a negative question: "won't you…?". Take the ません form and add か. It leaves the other person an easy way out, which is why it sounds polite. Careful: the positive 〜ますか only asks for information. To say yes: いいですね. To say no without saying "no": 〜は ちょっと…… (see Expression notes).

  • ノア: あした カフェで アイスクリームを たべませんか。Want to have ice cream at a café tomorrow?
  • きむらさん: こんばん うちで ばんごはんを たべませんか。 — ニコ: いいですね。Dinner at our place tonight? — Sounds good.
  • ケンタ: どようびに うめだで おちゃを のみませんか。Shall we have tea in Umeda on Saturday?

カフェに いきますか = "are you going to the café?" (a question). カフェに いきませんか = "would you like to go to the café?" (an invitation).

Frequency adverbs

Picture a thermometer: まいにち at the top, then よく, then ときどき, all with ます. Below zero live あまり ("not much") and ぜんぜん ("not at all"), and these two drag the verb into ません every time.

  • ノアは よく うめだに いきます。Noa often goes to Umeda.
  • ニコは あまり ハンバーガーを たべません。Nico doesn't eat hamburgers much.
  • ハチは ぜんぜん アイスクリームを たべません。Hachi never eats ice cream.

✗ぜんぜん たべます. あまり and ぜんぜん work like a warning: "what's coming is negative".

Word order

Build the sentence like a train: wagons of «noun + particle», and the verb is always the locomotive at the end. A handy default order for the wagons: topic は → (how often) → when → where で / to where に・へ → what を → verb. The particles carry the meaning, so the wagons can swap places; the locomotive can't.

  • ハナさんは あさ カフェで コーヒーを のみます。Hana has coffee at a café in the morning.
  • ノアは たいてい 十時(じゅうじ)ごろ としょかんへ いきます。Noa usually goes to the library around ten.
  • わたしは どようびに うちで えいがを みます。I'll watch a movie at home on Saturday.

うちで どようびに えいがを みます is also correct. Only the locomotive has a fixed place.

The topic particle は

は raises a flag: "now we're talking about this". The flag can stand on anything, not just on the person who acts: on a day (どようびは…), on an object (おちゃは…), on a place. A flag alone with a rising voice, «X は？», is a whole question: "and what about X?".

  • にちようびは あまり べんきょうしません。On Sundays, I don't study much.
  • ニコ: コーヒーは？ — ハナ: まいにち のみます。And coffee? — Every day.
  • おちゃは あまり のみません。Green tea, I don't drink much.

In おちゃは あまり のみません, おちゃ is the object, but は takes the place of を. Never put them together: ✗おちゃをは.
