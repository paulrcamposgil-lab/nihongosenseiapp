Grammar

The grammar points of lesson 4 and their examples, as the app explains them.

X があります — います · "There is…"

Two verbs for "there is": あります if the thing can't move by itself (a café, a book, a tree), います if it's alive and moves (Noa, Goma, a pigeon). Picture a sentence built from the outside in: first the place + に, then what you find there + が, then the verb. The same あります also covers "I've got (a thing)" and "(an event) is on": a class, a test, a part-time shift. います covers "I've got" for people and pets.

thingperson / animal
there is(lugar に) 〜が あります(lugar に) 〜が います
there isn't〜が ありません〜が いません
classある → u-verbいる → ru-verb
  • うちの ちかくに コンビニが あります。There's a convenience store near my place.
  • こうえんに ハチと ゴマが います。Hachi and Goma are in the park.
  • ノアは じてんしゃが ありません。Noa doesn't have a bicycle.

Quick test: can it walk away? → います. Plants don't, so はな → あります. And don't mix up が ありません ("there isn't / I haven't got") with じゃ ありません ("it isn't"): かさが ありません = no umbrella; かさじゃ ありません = that's not an umbrella.

Describing where things are

To place something, start from the landmark: X は [landmark] の [position word] です. Japanese says "the bookstore's side" (ほんやの となり), not "next to the bookstore". The position words are nouns, so they can take particles like any noun. For "between", name both landmarks with と: Y と Z の あいだ. If you describe an action happening there, the place takes で: ほんやの まえで まちました.

X は Y の … ですthe meaning
みぎ · ひだりto the right / left of Y
まえ · うしろin front of / behind Y
なか · うえ · したinside / on top of / under Y
ちかく · となりnear / next to Y
Y と Z の あいだbetween Y and Z
  • カフェ・モモは ぎんこうと ほんやの あいだです。Café Momo is between the bank and the bookstore.
  • ハナさんの コーヒーは つくえの うえです。Hana's coffee is on the desk.
  • ゴマは いすの したに います。Goma is under the chair.

となり sounds right for two things of the same kind side by side: two shops, two people, two houses. A bag "beside" a desk is better with よこ (you'll meet it later).

Past tense of です

Only the です at the end moves into the past; the noun before it stays exactly the same. Affirmative でした, negative じゃなかったです. In more formal speech you'll also hear じゃありませんでした, and in writing ではありませんでした.

ですaffirmativenegative
present〜です〜じゃないです
past〜でした〜じゃなかったです
  • ノアの クラスは かようびでした。Noa's class was on Tuesday.
  • ばんごはんは さかなでした。 とんかつじゃなかったです。Dinner was fish. It wasn't tonkatsu.
  • ケンタさんは こうこうの とき、いい がくせいじゃなかったです。Kenta wasn't a good student in high school.

✗がくせいだったでした: one past marker is enough, and it's always at the end.

Past tense of verbs

Good news: if you can make ます and ません (Lesson 3), you already have the past. Swap ます → ました and ません → ませんでした. Everything before that — the stem, the ru/u/irregular class — works exactly as before.

  • ノアは カフェの まえで さんじゅっぷん まちました。Noa waited thirty minutes in front of the café.
  • ケンタさんは 三時(さんじ)に きませんでした。Kenta didn't come at three.
  • ハナさんは きのう あまり ねませんでした。Hana didn't sleep much yesterday.

Each new verb comes with its own particle — learn them as a pair: (person) に あう, (language) が わかる, (thing) を かく. And まつ hides a sound change: まつ → まちます.

も — too

も = "too, also". Now it can go on anything that repeats: a second person who does the same thing, a second object, a second place, a second day. The rule is about what も does to the particle already there: it swallows は, が and を, but it sits behind に, で and the rest.

beforewith も
は / が / をも
に / で / …にも / でも / …
  • ノアは ほんを かいました。 ケンタさん**も** ほんを かいました。Noa bought a book. Kenta bought a book too.
  • ゴマは パンを たべました。 ノアの くつ**も** たべました。Goma ate some bread. She ate Noa's shoe too.
  • ノアは ほんやに いきました。 ゆうびんきょく**にも** いきました。Noa went to the bookstore. She went to the post office too.

✗くつをも, ✗ケンタさんはも. Time words without に simply add も: よるも ("in the evening too").

一時間 (duration)

"For how long" is just a number + じかん (hours) or ふん/ぷん (minutes), dropped into the sentence with no particle, usually right before the verb or the object. "About" = add ぐらい. "And a half" = はん straight after じかん. With both, はん comes first and ぐらい closes: いちじかんはんぐらい.

the meaning
いちじかんone hour
にじかんはんtwo and a half hours
さんじかんぐらいabout three hours
いちじかんはんぐらいabout an hour and a half
  • ケンタさんは こうえんで にじかんぐらい しゃしんを とりました。Kenta took photos in the park for about two hours.
  • ハナさんは にちようびに じゅうじかん ゲームを しました。Hana played games for ten hours on Sunday.
  • ニコは まいにち いちじかんはん にほんごを べんきょうします。Nico studies Japanese for an hour and a half every day.

さんじに = at three (a moment); さんじかん = for three hours (a stretch). ごろ goes with moments, ぐらい with amounts.

たくさん / たくさん

たくさん ("a lot") doesn't need の or a particle of its own. It can float in two spots: after the noun and its particle (しゃしんを たくさん), or right before the noun (たくさん しゃしんを). Both work with あります/います too.

  • ケンタさんは しゃしんを たくさん とりました。Kenta took a lot of photos.
  • こうえんに いぬが たくさん いました。There were a lot of dogs in the park.
  • きむらさんの うちに たくさん カメラが あります。There are a lot of cameras at Mr. Kimura's house.

In this course, stick to the two positions above; たくさんの + noun exists too but isn't practised here.

と — with / and

Think of と as a little rope. Between two nouns it ties them into a list: パンと コーヒー. After a person it ties that person to you: ケンタさんと いきました ("with Kenta"). The rope only holds nouns; linking whole actions needs a verb form you'll learn in Lesson 6.

  • ハナさんは パンと コーヒーを かいました。Hana bought bread and coffee.
  • ノアは ケンタさんと ほんやに いきました。Noa went to the bookstore with Kenta.
  • ニコは きむらさんの こどもと ゲームを しました。Nico played games with Mr. Kimura's child.

と is "with" a companion. "With" a tool (with a pen, with chopsticks) is another particle, later on.
